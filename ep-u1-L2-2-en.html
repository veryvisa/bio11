This is the brief on levels of organization. This brief walks through nine biological terms classifying life, from single cell to entire environment, providing exact definitions, memory hooks, and test questions to master the material. First, unicellular means made of only one cell. Uni means one, so one cellular. Bacterium? Unicellular? Yes, it's one cell. Multicellular means many cells working together. Multi means many. Name an organism? Plants, animal, mushroom, or tree. A tissue is similar cells doing the same job. A team of the same kind of cell. Group of similar cells? A tissue. An organ is a body part of several tissues working as a unit, like a tool. Is the heart an organ? Yes, using several tissues. An organ's system is organs on one team for a big job. Stomach and intestines? Digestive system. Second, levels of organization build small to large. Cats take over our offices. Cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism. Bigger, tissue, or organ? Organ. A population is members of one species in the same area. The people of one species. Forestier? A population. A community is different populations living together, many species as neighbors. An ecosystem is a community plus non-living things. Eco means home. What does it include? Abiotic factors. Life needs cells, energy, growth, reproduction, stimuli response, and homeostasis. Populations evolve. Cells form tissues. Tissues form organs. Organs form systems. Systems form organisms. Organisms form populations. Populations form communities. And adding water, soil, and sunlight creates an ecosystem. Finally, order smallest to largest. Organ, cell, organism, tissue. It's cell, tissue, organ, organism. Because levels build upward. Group of same species in same area? Population. Since it's one species in one place. Which level includes non-living factors such as soil and sunlight? The answer is ecosystem. An ecosystem is the community plus its non-living environment.
