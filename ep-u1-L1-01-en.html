This is the brief on characteristics of life. These notes break down the four essential biology terms that define what counts as a living organism. First is the organism. Any single living thing, such as a bacterium, a plant, or a fish. Here is your memory hook. An organism is organized. Its parts work together as one being. Quick check. Is a mushroom an organism? Yes, a fungus is a living thing. Second is the cell. The smallest unit that can carry out all the functions of life. The memory hook. Robert Hooke named cells after cella, small room, because they looked like tiny rooms. Quick check. What is the smallest unit of life? The cell. Third is homeostasis. Keeping the inside of the body steady even when the outside changes. The memory hook. Homeo means similar. Stasis means standing still. A similar state kept. Quick check. Why do you sweat when you're hot? To cool down and keep a steady body temperature. You know, homeostasis. Finally, metabolism covers all the chemical reactions in a cell that build molecules or release energy. The memory hook. Meta means change. Food is changed into energy. Quick check. Does a living cell use energy? Yes, metabolism uses and releases energy all the time. Let's summarize with a couple of test questions. Which is not usually listed as a characteristic of life? Being able to fly. Flying isn't shared by all living things. Metabolism, response, and reproduction are. Next up, a plant leans toward a window. The light from the window is the stimulus. Light is the change in the surroundings. So it is the stimulus. The leaning is the response.
