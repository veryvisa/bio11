This is the brief on Unit 7 review. Animals. Body plants. First, before looking inside, we gotta understand overall shape. Take bilateral symmetry. A body split into mirror image halves by one cut. Because bi means two, and lattice means side. This connects to cephalization. Gathering sense organs at the head, since cephal means head. Test question. Which symmetry goes with cephalization? Bilateral. Why? Well, if moving straight forward, bilaterally, you need those sensory headlights and bumpers right up front. Second, how does an embryo build internal tissues? It folds into a gastrula, creating germ layers. Ectoderm, ecto meaning outside, forms skin and nerves. Mesoderm, meso for middle, forms muscles. Endoderm, endo for inside, forms the gut. Test. Which layer forms the nervous system and skin? Ectoderm. These layers create a celum, pronounced C-lum. A true cavity entirely lined with mesoderm. Finally, how does the embryo's first hole dictate the animal? In a protostome, proto for first, stoma for mouth, the first opening becomes the mouth. In a deuterostome, or second mouth, the mouth forms second. Test question. In a deuterostome, the blastopor becomes the anus.
