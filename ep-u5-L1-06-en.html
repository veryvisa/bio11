Transcript讲稿文字

This is the brief on five related fungi terms. Zygote fungi, sac fungi, club fungi, ascus, and basidium. First, zygote and sac fungi. Zygote fungi, like bread mold, make resting zygote spores and spores inside structures called sporangia. To remember this, zygote means joined. Name a zygote fungus. Answer, bread mold, or rhizopus. Sac fungi make spores in sac-like asci, including yeasts, morels, and penicillium. Your memory hook is that ascus is Greek for wineskin, or sac. What is the sac called in sac fungi? An ascus. Second, club fungi. This group makes spores on club-shaped basidia, including mushrooms and puffballs. For a memory hook, basidia means little pedestal. Which group do mushrooms belong to? Club fungi. Now that we know the three groups, let's zoom in on the specific cells that actually form their spores. Finally, the spore-forming structures. An ascus is a sac-shaped cell in sac fungi where spores form. The plural is asci. Just remember, ascus means sac. What forms inside an ascus? Spores. Remember, basidia means little base. Where do club fungi spores form? On basidia. To summarize in one line. Zygote fungi include bread mold, sac fungi include yeast and penicillium, and club fungi include mushrooms. Which structure divides a hypha into cells? The answer is septum. Septa are cross walls. Which fungi makes spores in an ascus? The answer is sac fungi.
