This is the brief on evolutionary biology evidence and mechanisms. We cover the essential facts on how species develop, share underlying codes, and adapt for survival and reproduction, giving you the fundamental concepts needed. First, tracking early development brings us to slow down comparative embryology. This compares early development across species, looking very alike in related groups. Remember, embryo equals the earliest stage of development. Quick check. What does embryology show about vertebrates? Their early embryos look very similar, which points to a common ancestor. Second, the microscopic code is slow down molecular evidence. This means similarities in DNA or proteins between species, showing how closely related they are. Remember, molecule equals a tiny unit of matter. DNA is a molecule that keeps the record. Quick check. Which species shares more DNA with humans? A chimpanzee or a mouse? The chimpanzee. Third, species adapt via genetic traits like slow down antibiotic resistance. This is a bacteria's ability to survive an antibiotic, spreading by natural selection. Think anti equals against, bio equals life. Resistance equals a refusal to be killed. Quick check. Why does using antibiotics too often cause resistance? It kills the non-resistant bacteria, so the resistant ones survive and multiply. Finally, survival and reproduction leads to slow down sexual selection. This selects for traits helping an organism win a mate. Remember, sex equals about mates. Quick check. What does sexual selection favor? Traits that help an individual get mates. Test scenario. After a hurricane kills most of an island's lizards, the survivors have fewer alleles. The answer is the bottleneck effect. A sharp drop in population size. Which evidence compares DNA between species? The answer is molecular evidence. DNA and protein similarities show relatedness. The answer is molecular evidence.
