First, evolutionary upgrades. Early non-vascular plants like mosses lacked tubes, staying small and damp. How'd they become giants? They developed a vascular system. This sparked reproductive leaps.

Ferns use spores, gymnosperms rock cones with naked seeds, and angiosperms use flowers to protect seeds inside fruit. Angiosperms then split into monocots with one seed leaf and dicots with two. Second, let's look at plumbing and life cycles. Xylem carries water up from roots while phloem moves sugar. Just remember, phloem equals bark.

Sugar flows in the bark. Plants also undergo an alternation of generations, taking turns between haploid and diploid phases. Above ground, the waxy cuticle and stomata act like a waterproof rain jacket with adjustable vents for gas exchange. The male stamen makes pollen. The female carpal receives it.

Watch out for this test trap. After fertilization, the ovule becomes the seed, while the ovary ripens into fruit. Also, roots don't pump water up. It's transpiration, water evaporating from leaves, that physically pulls water up the xylem.
