This is the brief on biological characteristics of life and classification. These notes outline the basic levels of biological organization from a single cell up to an entire organism. First, we start with cells, which can be either unicellular or multicellular. Unicellular just means it's made of only one cell. Remember this memory hook. Uni equals one. One cellular. So, is a bacterium unicellular? Yes, it is one cell. On the flip side,

multicellular means it's made of many cells that work together. Multi equals many. Name a multicellular organism. Any plant, animal, or mushroom. For example, a tree. Second, let's step up to tissues and organs. A tissue is basically a group of similar cells that do the same job. A tissue is a team of the same kind of cell. What do we call a group of similar cells doing one job? A tissue. Now, an organ is a body part made of several tissues that works as one unit for a job.

An organ is a tool made of several tissues. Is the heart a tissue or an organ? An organ. It is made of several tissues. Finally, we have organ systems. An organ system is several organs that work together to do a big job for the body. System equals organs on one team. Name a system that includes the stomach and intestines. The digestive system. So, here's how the whole idea fits together. Cells form tissues. Tissues form organs. Organs form organ systems. And systems form an organism. Each level does a bigger

job. Put these in order from smallest to largest. Organ. Cell. Organism. Tissue. The answer is cell. Tissue. Organ. Organ. Organism. The levels go cell. Tissue. Organ. Organ. System. Organism. Which level includes nonliving factors such as soil and sunlight? The answer is ecosystem. An ecosystem is the community plus its nonliving environment.
