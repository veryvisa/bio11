First, let's talk invertebrates. Porifera are your pore-bearing sponges. Cnidarians, Greek for nettle, have sticking cells. Worms add complexity from cavity-less flatworms to segmented annelids. Molluscs literally mean soft, featuring a muscular foot and a cloak-like mantle.

Arthropods mean jointed feet. They go through metamorphosis and shed their chitin exoskeleton. Econoderms have spiny skin and a water vascular system. Quick test check? If asked which phylum has stinging cells, always pick nidaria.

And for a mantle, it's definitely a mollusk. Second, we transition to chordates, named for chorda or string, since they possess a flexible internal notochord. Tracing the land transition, fish use gills. Amphibians live a double life in water and land.

And reptiles adapt with dry skin. The amniotic egg was a literal game-changer, allowing embryos to finally develop on land. Just remember, a chitin exoskeleton is absolutely never a chordate feature. Finally, we look at mammals.

Ectotherms rely on outside heat, kind of like a sunroom, while endotherms generate inside heat, like a furnace. Placentals use a placenta for nutrients. Marsupials finish developing in a pouch. And monotremes actually lay eggs.
