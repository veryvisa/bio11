This is the brief on bacteria, archaea, eukarya, and prokaryotes. These notes from BC Life Sciences 11 cover the characteristics of life and classification, giving you the four essential terms you need to understand the domains of life. First, bacteria. We're talking about a domain of single-celled prokaryotes with no nucleus found almost everywhere.

Memory hook. One bacterium, many bacteria. Quick check. Do bacteria have a nucleus? Answer.

No, they are prokaryotes. Second, archaea. These single-celled prokaryotes kind of look like bacteria but are chemically different. Many actually live in extreme places. Memory hook.

Archaea equals ancient. Archaeology, study of ancient things. Quick check. Name a place where archaea may live. Answer.

Hot springs, very salty lakes, or deep sea vents. Third, eukarya. This is the domain of organisms whose cells have a nucleus like protists, fungi, plants, and animals. Memory hook. U equals true.

Carry equals nucleus. True nucleus. Quick check. Are humans in bacteria, archaea or eukaryotes. Are humans in bacteria, archaea or eukaryote?

Answer. Eukaryote. Finally, prokaryote. This is a cell or organism with literally no nucleus, so its DNA floats free inside the cell. Memory hook.

Pro equals before. Before the nucleus evolved. Quick check. Which domains are prokaryotes? Answer.

Bacteria. So, all life falls into three domains. Bacteria and archaea are prokaryotes with no nucleus, while eukaryocels have a nucleus. Which domain has cells without a nucleus and often lives in extreme places? The answer is archaea.

Archaea are prokaryotes that often live in extreme places. A mushroom absorbs food from dead matter. It is a? The answer is heterotroph. It cannot make its own food, so it is a heterotroph.
