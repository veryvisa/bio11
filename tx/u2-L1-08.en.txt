This is the brief on five key evolution terms. These notes provide a quick review of essential biology definitions, memory hooks, and check questions to help understand evolutionary structures and fossils. First, a fossil is the preserved remains or traces of an organism that lived long ago. The hook? Fossil comes from Latin fossilis, meaning dug up.

So, what is a fossil? The preserved remains or traces of an ancient organism. Second, a transitional fossil shows traits of both an older group and the group that came after it. Hook? Transition equals going across, a bridge fossil. Name a transitional fossil?

Tiktaalik, fish to tetrapod, or Archaeopteryx, dinosaur to bird. Third, a homologous structure is a body part alike in different species because they share a common ancestor. Hook? Homo equals same. Logos equals relation. Same origin. A human arm and batwing have the same bones. What structure is this? Homologous.

Fourth, an analogous structure is a body part with a similar job in different species, not from a common ancestor. The hook? Ana equals according to. Analogy? Similar job. Do a bird and insect wings show homology or analogy? Analogy. They fly but don't share a common ancestor structure.

Fifth, a vestigial structure is a reduced body part with little or no use now, but had use in ancestors. Hook? Vestigium equals footprint. A trace left behind. Whales have tiny hip bones. What do we call them? Vestigial structures? Finally, let's hit test questions. Microevolution is best described as...

A change in allele frequencies in a population. Small-scale change in allele frequencies. A human arm and a whale flipper have the same bones. These are homologous structures. Same underlying structure from a common ancestor.
