This is the brief on natural selection and adaptation. We're covering the seven fundamental terms from the BC Life Sciences 11 notes explaining how organisms survive, reproduce, and change over time. First, natural selection, fitness, and adaptation. Natural selection means individuals with traits suiting their environment survive and reproduce more. Nature selects through variation, inheritance, overproduction, and differential survival. Does it change individuals

or populations? Populations. Changing which traits are common. Next, fitness is survival and reproduction success. The hook, fit, means leaves more offspring, not strong. Is the strongest always the fittest? Nope, it's about offspring. Adaptation is an inherited survival trait. The hook, adapt equals fit to, fitting a body to its home. Is a stretching giraffe neck an adaptation? No, it must be inherited. Second, selective pressure and competition. Selective pressure is an environmental

factor affecting survival, pushing a population in one direction. Name one, predators or climate change. Competition is organisms using the same limited resource. Calm means together, petere is seek. Why? Not everyone gets enough, so better suited ones survive. Third, micro and macroevolution. Microevolution is small allele frequency changes in one population. Micro means small. Change in one population. Microevolution. Macroevolution is large changes over time like new species. Macro means large.

Origin of new species? Macroevolution. Tying it together. Natural selection takes variation, inheritance, more offspring born than can survive, and helpful traits surviving. Micro means small allele changes. Macro means big changes over time. Fourth, the test questions. Original source of new alleles? mutation, because selection only sorts them. Fitness means success at surviving and reproducing. An adaptation example? Thick fur passed from parents in cold climates. Natural selection acts directly on

individuals, though populations change. Microevolution is best described as small-scale change in allele frequencies. So, let's see.
