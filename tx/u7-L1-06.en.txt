These notes cover four foundational BC Life Sciences 11 terms, focusing strictly on definitions, memory hooks, and quick checks for retention. First up, we start with the most visible aspect of a body plan. It's physical sections. This is segmentation. It's a body built from repeating sections, like in earthworms and insects. Remember, segment equals a cut piece. So,

quick check. Name a segmented animal. The answer is an earthworm or an insect. Second, we shift to early embryonic development. Meet the protostome. That's an animal whose first opening in the embryo, the blastopore, becomes the mouth. The memory hook? Proto equals first. Stoma equals mouth. First mouth. Quick check. What does the blastopore become in a protostome? The answer is the mouth. Third, let's contrast that with the alternative evolutionary path, the deuterostome.

Here, the blastopore actually becomes the anus, and the mouth forms later. Your memory hook? Deutero equals second. Second mouth. Quick check. Are humans protostomes or deuterostomes? The answer is deuterostomes. Finally, let's zoom in on that early embryonic structure. The blastula is an early embryo, basically a hollow ball of cells. The memory hook is blast equals bud or germ. Quick check. What shape is a blastula? The answer is a hollow ball of cells. To recap,

animal body plans are defined by their segmentation, the fate of early embryonic openings, and structures like the hollow blastula. Let's hit the final test questions. A jellyfish shows.
