This is the brief on biological classification. Today, we are reviewing the taxonomic ranks used to classify life from broad to narrow, and looking at how to read evolutionary trees. First, kingdom and phylum. So, a kingdom is a huge group just below domain. Kingdom equals a big realm, like animals or plants. Which kingdom do humans belong to? Animalia, or animals. Down a rank is phylum, where animals share a basic body plan. Phylum equals tribe, plural phyla. Chordata equals animals with a nerve cord. What is the plural of phylum? Phyla. Second, class, order, and family. Class is right below phylum. Mammals are one class. Class sits between phylum and order. Which class are humans in? Mammalia, mammals. Right below that is order. Primates are one order. Order sits between class and family. Which order includes monkeys, apes, and humans? Primates. Then comes family, made of related genera. Family sits just above genus. Which rank is just below family? Genus. Finally, putting it all together with cladograms. From broad to narrow, the ranks are domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species. Dear King Philip came over for good soup. On cladograms, each branch point is a common ancestor. Species sharing a more recent common ancestor are more closely related. But be careful. Read the tree strictly by branch points, not by tip order. Let's test it out. Put these in order from smallest to largest. Organ. Cell. Organism. Tissue. The answer is cell, tissue, organ, organism. The levels go cell, tissue, organ, organ, system, organism. Next, in the name Homo sapiens. Sapiens is the species. The second part of a scientific name is the species.
