First up, the three groups. Zygote fungi make zygospores in sporangia. Hook. Zygo equals joined. Quick check. Name a zygote fungus? Bread mold. Sac fungi make spores in sac-like ascii. Hook. Ascus is Greek for wineskin. Quick check. What's the sac called in sac fungi? An ascus. Club fungi make spores on club-shaped basidia. Hook. Basidium equals little pedestal. Quick check. Which group do mushrooms belong to? Club fungi. Second, let's hit spore parts. Ascus. A sac-shaped cell making spores. Hook. Ascus equals sac. Quick check. What forms inside an ascus? Spores. Basidium. A club-shaped cell making spores. Hook. Basidium equals little base. Quick check. Where do club fungi spores form? On basidia. Gills. Thin plates under caps holding spore cells. Hook. Gills like a fish's gills. Thin plates. Quick check. Where are a mushroom's gills? Under the cap. Septum. Cross wall dividing hyphae. Hook. Septum equals fence. Quick check. What does a septum do? Divides the thread into cells. Finally, nutrient cycling. Decomposers return dead matter nutrients to soil. Hook. Cycle equals goes round and round. Quick check. How do fungi help? Decomposing matter returning nutrients. To tie these terms together, the three fungus groups are zygote fungi, including bread mold, sac fungi, including yeast and penicillium, and club fungi, including mushrooms. Which structure divides a hypha into cells? The answer is septum. Septa are cross walls. Which fungi make spores in an ascus? The answer is sac fungi. Mushrooms belong to which group?
